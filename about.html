The evidence base

An outcomes-oriented synthesis.

Program evaluation is inaccessible and underleveraged. As a service, its practitioners are disparate or overpriced. As a practice, its methods are too niche, its implementation too resource-intensive, and its benefits too obscure for program teams to take on alone. The evidence says evaluation works best through long-term collaboration that builds trust, deepens context and shepherds adoption—and in the current market, those carry impossible costs for small and mid-sized organizations. Ascent is structured to make that relationship affordable.

Implementation science

The study of how proven practices take hold in real programs, and of the people who help them take hold.

Evaluation capacity building

The practice of making evaluation, and the use of what it finds, an ordinary part of how an organization works.

  1. 1997Evaluation capacity building

    Learning from the process

    Process use names the learning that comes from taking part in an evaluation, beyond anything in its findings (Patton).

  2. 2002Evaluation capacity building

    Capacity by design

    Evaluation capacity building is defined as a field: designed practice rather than a by-product, with the use of evaluation built into what capacity means (Stockdill, Baizerman & Compton).

  3. 2008Evaluation capacity building

    Capacity lives in culture

    A multidisciplinary model ties evaluation capacity to organizational learning and culture, and sets out the conditions for practice that lasts (Preskill & Boyle).

  4. 2009Implementation science

    A framework for what shapes implementation

    The Consolidated Framework for Implementation Research organizes the factors that help or hinder putting evidence into practice (Damschroder et al.).

  5. 2012Evaluation capacity building

    The field takes stock

    A research synthesis consolidates the literature, codes its outcomes at the individual and organizational levels, and identifies weaknesses in its designs (Labin et al.).

  6. 2013Implementation scienceCited by both fields

    Dose matters

    A two-year cluster-randomized trial finds dose-dependent gains across four capacity scales, greatest where technical assistance hours were highest (Acosta, Chinman et al.).

  7. 2013Evaluation capacity building

    Organizations, not individuals

    Instrument validation establishes that leadership, resources and learning climate decide whether individual knowledge ever becomes mainstreamed practice (Taylor-Ritzler et al.).

  8. 2014Evaluation capacity building

    Doing is not using

    The capacity to do evaluation is separated from the capacity to use it: methodological skill on one side, the organizational conditions that let findings have influence on the other (Cousins et al.).

  9. 2015Evaluation capacity building

    A habit of mind

    Evaluative thinking is named as the lasting disposition that evaluation capacity building aims to instill (Buckley et al.).

  10. 2016The sector

    The affordability gap, measured

    Roughly ninety-eight percent of small nonprofits and eighty percent of organizations with budgets above five million dollars have no dedicated evaluation staff (Innovation Network).

  11. 2017Implementation science

    Implementation support becomes a field of research

    Two intermediary organizations, one in the USA and one in Ireland and Northern Ireland, begin studying the role and competencies of the people who support implementation (NIRN and the Centre for Effective Services).

  12. 2018Implementation scienceCited by both fields

    Repetition and duration drive results

    A replication trial finds no significant effect in year one and small significant improvement after a second-year quality improvement cycle (Chinman, Acosta et al.).

  13. 2020Implementation science

    Competencies, described

    The Implementation Support Practitioner Profile and Practice Guide set out fifteen core competencies in three domains: co-creation and engagement, ongoing improvement, and sustaining change (Metz, Burke, Albers et al.).

  14. 2020Evaluation capacity building

    Relationship, not training

    Evaluation practice persists years after an intervention, and the persistence tracks ongoing evaluator contact rather than the initial training (Wade & Kallemeyn).
